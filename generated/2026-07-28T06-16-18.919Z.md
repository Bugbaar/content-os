Building AI agents in production is not just about deploying models. It requires a deep integration of engineering rigor, reliability, and scalability. Too often the focus is on the novelty of the AI itself, but the real challenge lies in operationalizing these systems within complex environments.

From my experience, three key engineering considerations stand out. First, designing for observability is crucial. Without granular monitoring and clear metrics, identifying when an AI agent deviates or degrades becomes guesswork rather than insight. Second, continuous feedback loops must be embedded. Real-world data evolves, and so should the models powering the agents. This means building pipelines that support retraining and validation without interrupting service. Third, managing dependencies is often underestimated. AI agents rely on multiple data sources, external APIs, and infrastructure components. Ensuring these interdependencies are robust and fault-tolerant makes the difference between resilience and downtime.

Moreover, collaboration between data scientists and engineers must move beyond handoffs. Shared ownership of deployment, testing, and incident response creates a sustainable path to production readiness.
